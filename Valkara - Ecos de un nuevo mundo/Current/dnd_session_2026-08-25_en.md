# D&D Session Summary: The Fair Seam and the Hall of a Thousand Threads

## Summary
With Romina bound in the tower and Kenshin off to face the Tejedora, the rest of the party debated waiting versus exploring Ilvana, chose the latter, and stumbled into a boarding house called *Posada la Costura Justa*, where they traded a day's labor as waiters, bartender, and stockroom help for a room and three meals. Breakfast service went smoothly and taught them the city's rituals - but when Fabio was sent out for fruit he vanished, and Mickey, Kalen, and Cael, searching for him one by one, were each intercepted by Readers and *tejedores* and marched into an identical underground chamber, forced into a chair, and bound in living gold thread while their "thread" was read into the city's *trama* - Kalen's, chillingly, turning out to be to unravel the entire city. Only Kenshin, bolstered by some hidden strength from Naut inside his pendant, tore free of his bindings and woke in a vast circular hall lined with thousands of identical bound figures, where he cut loose Mickey, Fabio, Kalen, Cael, Erios, and finally Romina herself. Climbing toward the tower's upper floors, the freed party triggered a vicious thread-trap that nearly killed Kenshin, worked out a color-coded floor puzzle tied to the city's ranks of robes, and burst through the final door straight into three *tejedores* having lunch - initiative was rolling as the session cut off.

---

## Key Events

### 1. The Posada la Costura Justa
* **A city without a market:** With Kenshin gone to seek the *Tejedora* and Romina still missing, the remaining five debated whether to wait a day for word of Kenshin or go looking for the market themselves; they chose to explore, wandering into a residential district where every building was identical down to the roofline.
* **A sign worth reading:** A shopfront reading *Posada la Costura Justa* ("The Fair Seam") caught their eye as the first building in the city with any real decoration, and they went inside over some grumbling about killing the adventuring spirit by asking directions instead of finding things themselves.
* **Working for a room:** Broke and unwilling to sell Fabio's mount *Chimuelo*, the party offered the innkeeper, a warm sixty-year-old woman named Reselvain, a day's labor in exchange for lodging; she agreed, assigning Cael and Kalen as waiters, Mickey to the bar, and Fabio - barred from the dining room for lack of a uniform - to the stockroom, paying modest wages she'd mostly claw back as rent.
* **Learning the ritual:** Reselvain drilled them on Ilvana's customs: every interaction opens with *"para qué vienes"* ("what do you come for") and closes with *"que encuentres tu función"* ("may you find your function"), and everyone's life is woven into a single citywide *trama* ("the Weave") by the thread they carry.

### 2. Breakfast Service and the Weave's Machinery
* **A smooth first seating:** Cael and Kalen served two tables of unnervingly polite guests without incident, while Mickey, digging up a hidden recipe book, prepared a precise root tea after a diner sent one back for a technicality.
* **Meeting Maren:** The chef, a large man named Maren, ran the kitchen alone and unbothered by anything, including Kalen's chaotic machete-style fruit chopping for the coming lunch service.
* **Readers versus weavers:** Reselvain, questioned about the city's constant scrutiny, explained that *lectores* (in blue) read a person's thread in the *trama* and escort mismatched threads onward, while *tejedores* (in green) are the ones who actually re-weave a thread into place - and mentioned, in passing, that her late husband had been a *lector* himself.

### 3. One by One, Bound to the Chair
* **Fabio disappears:** Sent to fetch fruit after the stockroom's barrels turned up empty, Fabio was intercepted en route by a Reader who declared his thread "confused in the *trama*," took his money and errand slip, and led him into one of the tall triangular buildings that ring the city.
* **The search that wasn't:** Kalen and Mickey, sent after him, were separately waylaid the same way - a Reader for Kalen, then a green-robed *tejedor* with a ledger of everyone's last known "reading" for Kalen again after he pressed for answers - and Cael met the identical fate moments later while looking for all three.
* **A chair for everyone:** Each of them, alone but seemingly in the same place and moment, was led down into an identical white stone chamber, told to sit in a narrow chair, and wrapped in living gold thread as a *tejedor* read their intent aloud; Fabio's lunge at his captor dissolved into an inexplicable calm under a failed will save, while Kalen was told flatly that his thread's true function in Ilvana was to *"completely destabilize the city."*
* **Swallowed by the thread:** One after another - Fabio, Mickey, Cael, and finally Kalen - was cocooned completely and dropped into a silent, paralyzed but conscious void, with no memory of how the others around them had fared.

### 4. Kenshin's Escape and the Hall of a Thousand Threads
* **A voice from the pendant:** Alone in his own version of the chair, Kenshin refused to sit and, drawing on some unexplained help from Naut - dormant since retreating into his pendant weeks earlier - tore the golden thread apart with his blade before it could fully bind him.
* **Miles of identical chairs:** He came to in a colossal circular hall lined with what looked like thousands of chairs, each holding a cocooned, thread-wrapped figure, with stairways leading both up and down at the far end.
* **Freeing the party:** Cutting threads like butter with his sword, Kenshin freed Mickey first, then recognized and freed Fabio and Kalen nearby, and then Romina - still bound since her capture the week before - and, working outward at random, Cael and Erios as well.
* **A vow of vengeance:** Fabio argued for freeing every captive in the hall out of principle, but the party, with time against them, chose to press on instead; a furious Kalen, freshly told his own thread was meant to unmake the city, vowed they'd tear Ilvana down themselves.

### 5. The Trap of Colors and an Interrupted Lunch
* **A trap dressed as decoration:** Climbing past more sleeping captives, the group reached an antechamber with a golden carpet, two ornamental bushes, and a small white statue before a stone door - beyond which lay a puzzle room floored in colored tiles and flanked by two murals of a glowing, golden-robed woman the party immediately connected to Naut's missing sister.
    * Kenshin's first step onto a tile triggered a savage thread-trap that sliced rather than bound him, and the party spent several tense rounds hacking, dissolving, and prying the threads loose - Fabio's acid-slicked blade, Mickey's shattered ice constructs - before Romina finally healed his wounds.
    * Trial and error revealed a sequence puzzle keyed to the city's robe hierarchy: white was safe, orange lashed out at Fabio, and red very nearly dragged Romina away entirely before Chimuelo snatched her clear - until Cael spotted a third, faded statue beside the known blue (*lectores*) and green (*tejedores*) figures and guessed at a hidden rank.
* **The door clicks open:** Stepping in careful sequence - white, blue, green, and finally red - the party pressed all four plates at once, and the door swung open with an audible click.
* **Lunch, interrupted:** Beyond it sat three *tejedores* mid-meal, who dropped their food the instant they spotted the party and rolled for initiative - Mickey moved to trap them in a conjured wall of water to freeze them where they sat as the fight, and the session, broke off.

## Open Questions
* **What does it mean that Kalen's thread is "to destabilize the city"?** A *tejedor* stated it as plain fact moments before binding him - is this Ilvana's paranoid reading of him, or a genuine glimpse of his true role in whatever comes next?
* **What happens to the thousands of bound figures in that hall?** The party freed only their own, leaving countless other cocooned people behind - are they prisoners, sleepers, or something already lost, and will the party's choice to leave them come back to matter?
* **How did Naut help Kenshin break free?** He's been dormant inside his pendant for weeks, yet something of him reached Kenshin at exactly the right moment - is he stronger than he let on, or was this a cost he can't easily pay again?
* **What is the hidden third rank the party guessed at?** Blue *lectores* and green *tejedores* are known quantities, but a faded, unfamiliar statue and a puzzle tile keyed to it suggest a rank - or a fallen one - the party hasn't met yet.
* **Are they close to the Tejedora now?** Two staircases, a lethal puzzle door, and three ambushed *tejedores* all sit near the tower's upper reaches - is she finally within reach, and what will three battle-ready weavers plus whatever's above them cost the party to get there?
